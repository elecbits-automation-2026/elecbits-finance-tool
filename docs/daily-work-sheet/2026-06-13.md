## 1. Reorder budget approval chain to Finance-first
**Commit:** `168744f`

Move the Finance Head review to immediately after the department head, so
budgets route **Dept → Finance → VP → CEO** instead of Dept → VP → CEO →
Finance. Finance now signs off first and the executive chain escalates on top
by amount (≥1L adds VP, ≥5L adds VP then CEO). The same Finance-first reorder
is applied to Payments and POs; Payments keep their straight-to-CEO routing at
≥5L (no VP) by design.

- `workflow.ts` `computeNextStage`: relocate FinanceHead ahead of VP/CEO for
  all three flows; the terminal transition moves to the highest authority the
  amount requires.
- **Migration 0012** redefines `budget_next_stage()`; `schema.sql` updated to
  match. Per-stage authorization (`can_act_on_budget`, `budgets_guard`) is
  order-independent and unchanged.
- Form-flow previews (Budget/Payment/PO) reordered to show Finance first.
- Test-harness expected sequences + RLS B1 chain updated; audit-doc routing
  tables refreshed and the "VP skipped at ≥5L" finding marked resolved.

## 2. Fire SuperManager fast-track at the now-mid-chain FinanceHead stage
**Commit:** `5b0e617`

The Finance-first reorder made FinanceHead a **mid-chain** stage, but
`ActionButtons.isEarlyStage` still excluded it. So a SuperManager approving a
≥1L budget at FinanceHead fell through to the normal path and advanced to VP —
but the `budgets_guard` trigger expects *any* SuperManager approval to jump
straight to Active, so that write was rejected server-side (invalid transition
`FinanceHead → VP`).

- Drop FinanceHead from the `isEarlyStage` exclusion list (its only use is the
  SM-override gate) so the fast-track fires there for all three flows: budgets
  → Active, payments/POs → Accountant.
- Add **RLS regression B5**: a SuperManager at FinanceHead must fast-track to
  Active; the VP transition the buggy client would send is proven rejected.
